ORBITAL ANTE - PLAYTEST BUILD (working title)
Built 1 Nov 2026 from the phase-2-player branch (Phase 8 slice, the home base, your control fixes, the Moon buggy and hunter swarms). Needs Godot 4.7.2 exactly.

OPEN IT ON THE STEAM DECK (Desktop Mode)
1. Unzip this file somewhere easy, for example ~/OrbitalAnte-playtest
   (Dolphin file manager: right-click the zip > Extract > Extract archive here).
2. Start Godot 4.7.2 (~/Applications/Godot/Godot_v4.7.2-stable_linux.x86_64).
3. In the Project Manager click Import, open the unzipped
   OrbitalAnte-playtest folder, pick project.godot, then click Import / Open.
4. The first open imports the models and takes a minute or two. If you see a
   few red "Cannot open file ... fontdata" lines during that first import,
   they are harmless; close and reopen the project and they go away.
5. Press F5 (or the Play button, top right) to start. Click Launch, pick
   your contracts on the board, then press Launch.

Everything is in this one folder; the compiled game code is in bin/.
If you imported an older copy of this zip, remove it from the Project
Manager list first and delete its folder.

CONTROLS (controller or mouse and keyboard)
Move: left stick / WASD      Look: right stick / mouse
Jump, jetpack up: A / Space
Crouch: tap B / Ctrl or C to stay crouched (tap again or jump to stand),
  hold it to crouch only while held, or click the left stick to toggle.
  In the air B / Ctrl is jetpack down.
Interact: X / E              Map: M, zoom with PgUp/PgDn or mouse wheel
Frame-time overlay: F3 (keyboard only now)   Free the mouse cursor: Esc
Cash out (buckled in, target reached): hold View / Enter

COMBAT (on foot)
Fire: RT / left mouse        Aim down sights: LT / right mouse
Melee (the bat if you have one, otherwise a shove): LB / F
Rope gun: RB / B key         Swap rifle and rock: Y / Tab
Reload: R, or X / E when there's nothing else to use
Pick up a dropped gun (swaps yours), or pick up / put down a tied
soldier: X / E while looking at it
(LT still matches velocity with the jetpack when you're off the ground.)

WHAT IS IN THIS BUILD
- Walking on Earth with planet gravity, suit oxygen, jetpack and tether.
- The full moving solar system with 14 bodies and a live map.
- You start at the home base on Earth. The starter lander waits on the
  launch pad beside the gantry, straight ahead up the lit path. Stand under its
  belly ring and the hatch opens and lifts you in. In the cabin, look at
  the seat and press X / E to buckle in.
    Flying: left stick / WASD thrust, A / Space up, B / Ctrl down,
    right stick / mouse to turn, LB / RB (Z / X keys) to roll,
    hold LT / Shift to match velocity (brakes to a hover).
    The dash screen shows speed, climb rate, altitude, a drift ball (where
    you're sliding), fuel and oxygen. F4 switches between its three styles.
    Y / V flicks through the landing camera and two selfie-stick cameras.
    Target lock: look at a planet or moon and press D-pad up / L. Brackets
    mark it (an arrow at the screen edge if it's off-screen) and the dash
    shows its distance and closing speed. Press again on it to unlock.
    Autopilot: with a target locked, press RT / Q. The lander turns, burns,
    steers round any planet in the way, brakes and stops 120 m above the
    target, then holds there until you touch the controls. Earth to the
    Moon takes about 35 seconds. Any stick input takes back control.
  Press X / E again to unbuckle, look at the floor hatch and press X / E to open it,
  step onto it to ride down. Crouch (B / C) to duck out under the ship.
- Fixed: the side of the Moon facing Earth was almost weightless; the
  Moon's own gravity now takes over near it.
- Takeoff effects: the engine plume, a smoke cloud in the ground's colour,
  flying grit, a flickering engine light and controller rumble. They're
  biggest on the pad and fade as you climb. No sounds yet.
- Crashes: hit the ground hard (over about 4 m/s) and the parts that hit
  take damage, with sparks. Keep battering a part and it breaks off and
  tumbles away for good. A damaged engine pushes less, damaged thrusters
  turn slower, and the dash shows hull health. Stay buckled in: a hard
  crash while you're standing up can kill you.
- Repair: go outside, look at a damaged leg, thruster, engine or antenna
  and hold X / E. The prompt names the part and a bar fills. Parts that
  broke off are gone. No repair sound yet.

NEW IN THIS BUILD (your fixes and the Phase 8 slice)
- Hunter swarms: while you're out on a surface (on foot or in the buggy,
  below about 60 m), packs of Venera hunters come for you. First you get a
  "Hunters inbound!" note and red flare columns 45-70 m away where they
  will land; 4 seconds later they drop in and run straight at you,
  re-finding you every few seconds. Packs get bigger and come more often
  each ante: 2-3 hunters every ~3 min at ante 1, up to 10-14 every minute
  at ante 8 (and more in Endless), with more tier 2s later. To shake them,
  get aboard a ship and leave, or get far away (they give up past ~320 m).
  The home base is a safe zone, and there's a grace period at the start of
  each ante.
- Moon buggy: one is parked beside the launch pad at home, and one waits
  near the Moon's landing area. Walk up and press X / E to climb in.
  Left stick (WASD) drives and steers, A (Space) fires the rocket boost,
  B (C) is the handbrake for drifts, right stick (mouse) looks around,
  X / E climbs out. In the air the stick tilts the buggy so you can land
  it. If it ends up on its roof, hold A (Space) to roll it back over.
  Hard crashes hurt you (it's an open seat), and enemies hear the motor.
  While driving, the bottom-right gauges show your speed and boost fuel.
- Baseball bat: one lies on a supply crate in the Venera base on the
  Moon. Look at it and press X / E to take it as your melee weapon (LB / F).
- Controls: the right stick now has a response curve (small tilts aim
  finely, full tilt is full speed), up/down is a bit slower than
  left/right, and holding the stick fully over for a moment speeds the turn
  up for quick about-faces. Crouch is tap-to-toggle (see Controls).
- Lander: the legs are 1.1 m taller on telescoping struts, so you can walk
  straight under the belly to the hatch ring. The front windows are bigger
  and lower, and the dash screen lies back on the console below them, so
  you can see out from the seat.
- Home base: a launch pad with the lander on it, the NASA gantry, a
  hangar, a control tower, two crew habs, the locker building, fuel tanks,
  a tracking dish and lamp-lit paths, on ground graded flat.
- More terrain features on every planet and moon (mesas, volcanoes,
  canyons, scarps, dunes, craters and more).
- Moon dishes: two big dishes stand on the Moon. Walk to the control
  cabinet beside one and hold X / E to switch it on (120 points; switching
  on both adds 400).
- Damaged satellite: a relay satellite with three sparking orange panels
  orbits low over the Moon. Fly up, match speed, clip your tether on and
  hold X / E on each panel (300 points when all are fixed). Its restored
  signal brings a Venera patrol down to the surface below it.
- Venera base: the Moon camp now has hab pods, low walls to hide behind,
  supply crates and a radio mast with a blinking light. It moves with the
  camp each ante.
- New contract: "Get the Moon talking" (switch on dishes or fix the relay).
- Balance: upgrades cost half as much, discovery/repair/salvage values grow
  10% per ante, and later ante targets are higher (simulated, untested by
  players).

NEW IN PHASE 7 (the ante loop)
- A run is now a set of timed antes. Each ante starts on a contract board:
  three optional jobs (risk 1 to 3, riskier ones pay more), take up to two.
  Press Launch and the 12-minute clock starts. Ante 1's target is 1,000.
- Top left: the ante, the clock (turns red under 2 minutes), your Ante
  Score against the target with a count-up and a bar, the multiplier
  (x1.0 and up) and a pace meter (ahead / on pace / behind). Your taken
  contracts and their progress sit under it.
- Points: kills (grunt 25, veteran 60), limb shots +5, disarms +15, a camp
  clear 150 (every soldier down or tied up), first landing on a body 80,
  and first discoveries of big landforms and lava tubes (30-80 each, plus
  20 extra seconds on the clock). Repeating the same thing pays a bit less
  each time; mixing kinds of score raises the multiplier, and so does
  chaining actions within 20 seconds of each other.
- Notifications slide in top right (four at most; repeats count up).
- Hit the target and you can keep going, or buckle into the lander and hold
  View / Enter to cash out: every second left on the clock turns into
  Wallet points. Run out of time below the target and the run is over.
- Between antes: the upgrade screen. Buy attribute levels (Damage, Speed
  and jump, Oxygen, Defense) with your Wallet, pick one of three perks
  (reroll for a price that goes up each time), and scratch your free
  ticket (its odds are printed on it). Unspent Wallet earns 10% interest.
  Five perks work in this build: Deep Breath, Ghost, Glass Cannon, Second
  Wind and Overclocked; the rest need later systems (rovers, salvage,
  chests, bounties) and are held back.
- Each new ante the Moon camp is manned again in a new spot.
- The run summary shows how far you got and your local leaderboard
  (best antes and points, with the seed). Dying also ends the run there.

NEW IN PHASE 6 (combat, vertical slice)
- A camp of four Venera Collective soldiers (working faction name) patrols
  a short walk from where you land on the Moon: three light troopers with
  rifles who rush you, and one bigger tier 2 with a laser double-barrel
  who flanks. A yellow "?" means one heard or glimpsed something; a red
  "!" means they've seen you and will open fire about 0.7 s later. Lose
  them and they search the last place they saw you for about 45 s, then
  go back to patrolling. Crouching makes you slower to spot.
- You start with the rifle. Shoot an arm to disarm (the gun drops; walk
  over a gun like yours for its ammo, or press X / E to swap guns), shoot
  a leg off to drop them, headshots hurt double. Limbs come off as gibs,
  and the dead become ragdolls. Disarmed soldiers run at you and punch.
- The laser double-barrel (take it from the tier 2) gibs at close range.
  The rock is infinite, pathetic and funny. No baseball bat is placed in
  the world yet; it's in the game for the bounty/loot pass.
- Rope gun: 3 shots for the whole run, and misses count. A roped soldier
  drops its gun and is wrapped in rope. Press X / E to pick it up (it rides
  under your arm) and again to put it down. Delivering targets is a later
  phase.
- HUD: weapon and ammo bottom right (with rope shots left), hit marker
  ticks at the crosshair (white hit, orange limb off, red kill), and a red
  arc pointing to whoever just shot you.
- Gore is on by default; the toggle comes with the options menu. No combat
  sounds yet.

NEW IN PHASE 5 (planet surfaces)
- The Moon has its full surface kit: two dark mare plains (smooth, fast
  driving ground), a big multi-ring basin on the far side, a complex crater
  with a craggy central peak, winding rilles and wrinkle ridges on the mare,
  crater chains, two young craters with bright rays, and two dark, frosty
  craters that never see the sun (one at the south pole, one just off the
  north pole near where you land).
- Lava tubes: the Moon has two deep pits on its mare plains. Drop into one
  with the jetpack and look for the dark opening in the pit wall. Walk in:
  the tube winds under the plain to a wider chamber at the end. Your helmet
  lamp switches on by itself in the dark and off again in daylight. Use the
  jetpack to climb back out of the pit.
- Seeded features (crater chains, rilles, pits and caves) now move between
  runs with the run seed. The big landmarks stay put every run.

- Mars: a huge dead shield volcano, a mega-canyon with a side branch and an
  outflow channel, mesas stepping down into buttes and hoodoos, a canyon
  maze, wind-carved yardang ridges, splash-rimmed rampart craters, and an
  icy south polar cap with spiral troughs.
- Mars geysers: on the south polar cap, vents puff white frost for a few
  seconds, then blast a dark dust column. Stand on one when it goes and it
  throws you about 20 m up. Every eruption is warned first (no sound yet).
- Phobos: one giant crater (always in the same place), long grooves and
  chains of pits.
- Weather (seeded per run, and every storm builds up slowly so you see it
  coming): Mars dust storms that hide the view and push you around (much
  less while you stand on the ground), and dust devils you can walk into;
  Earth showers, thunderstorms with blue-white lightning, and snow; Venus
  acid storms with green lightning that slowly eat your ship's hull (never
  below a quarter on their own, so leave and repair); Titan orange haze and
  methane drizzle. Storms dim the sun. Weather never reaches inside the
  ship's cabin or a cave.
- New surfaces: Mercury (a big ring basin, long cliff scarps, frosty dark
  polar crater), Venus (giant volcanoes, ring-shaped coronae, flat pancake
  domes, lava channels), Io (glowing lava-lake pits), Europa (jumbled ice
  blocks of chaos terrain, twin-crested ice ridges), Titan (a big methane
  sea, long dunes), Earth (mesas with buttes and hoodoos, flooded sinkholes).
- Gas giants: dive under Jupiter's, Saturn's or Uranus's cloud deck. The
  sky turns the gas colour and the sun dims. Jupiter has ice-blade fields
  (penitentes) and red lightning, Saturn a big polar basin and gold
  lightning, Uranus a giant cliff and green lightning.
- Neptune is an ocean world. You land on a dry island at the pole. Swim
  down with the jetpack: the water holds you up and slows you, gets darker
  and murkier the deeper you go, and purple glows flash in the dark. Your
  suit oxygen runs under water. Black-smoker vents on the sea-floor ridge
  shimmer, then shove you upward. Earth's lakes and Titan's methane lakes
  are swimmable too.
